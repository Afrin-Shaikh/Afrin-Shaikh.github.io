Clustering university students by study behaviour

Research internship at Krea University under Prof. Brijesh Kumar Jha. Question: do students fall into distinct study-and-performance profiles that CGPA alone hides? Data: a public Mendeley survey of 1,194 students with 31 fields.

What I did

  • Filled the single missing field with the mode, standardised text answers, and converted mixed-format numbers (ranges, fractions, percentages) to one scale.
  • Built two indicators: High Performance (CGPA above 3.0) and Study Efficiency (CGPA divided by daily study hours).
  • Encoded categories, scaled with MinMaxScaler, and ran K-Means with k = 3 on 1,185 students. Nine students who reported 0 study hours dropped out because efficiency is undefined for them.

How reliable are these groups?

I scored k = 2 to 6 with the silhouette measure (higher means better-separated clusters).

A score near 0.23 means the groups overlap a lot. I chose k = 3 because three groups are easy to interpret, not because the data strongly favours it. k = 4 scores slightly higher.

Auditing my own results

When I rebuilt this for the portfolio, I checked the output against my original report and found problems.

Labels did not match the numbers. My report called the largest group "Efficient High Achievers" and the 12.4% group "Developing Students, at risk". The data shows the opposite: the largest group has the lowest CGPA, and the 12.4% group has the highest CGPA, the highest attendance and a 1-hour study habit. That group is not at risk by grades. The table above uses what the data shows.

  • A scaling error. One attendance entry written as a range ("94-98") became 96 on a scale where everyone else is 0 to 4. MinMax scaling then squashed everyone else's attendance near zero, so attendance had almost no say in the clusters. My cleaning function averaged ranges without converting them.
  • Group C is defined by one value. Every student in it studies exactly 1 hour a day, and dividing CGPA by 1 inflates the efficiency score. The ratio feature rewards low study hours more than it measures efficiency.
  • Dropped records. Dividing by 0 study hours removed 9 students, and I had not reported that.

What I would do next

  • Fix the range conversion, cap outliers, and add an automatic range check to the cleaning step. I have not re-run the model with these fixes yet.
  • Replace the ratio with a measure that does not blow up at 1 hour, and test whether groups stay stable across random seeds and across k = 3 and 4.
  • Report cluster labels only after checking them against the averages.
